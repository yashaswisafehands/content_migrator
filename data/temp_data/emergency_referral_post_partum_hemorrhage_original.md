# Chapter: Post-Partum Haemorrhage

### Emergency referral - postpartum haemorrhage

> EMERGENCY!

Do not transfer if the emergency is not controlled!

Never transfer a patient with uncontrolled bleeding, retained placenta or shock.

After emergency management and when her condition is stable, discuss the decision to transfer with the woman and her relatives.

Quickly organize transport and possible financial aid.

Inform the referral centre.

Accompany the woman and her newborn.

It is crucial that the woman is accompanied by a healthcare professional who is trained in delivery care and is able to manage an emergency during transportion.

Consider bringing a relative who can donate blood.

Transfer the newborn with the mother.

### Bring

Referral note.

## Essential emergency drugs:

- **Oxytocin** and **ergometrine** (alternatively **misoprostol**) for treatment of post-partum haemorrhage.
- **Hydralazine** for IV treatment of elevated blood pressure.
- **Magnesium sulphate** for eclampsia.
- **Calcium gluconate** for intoxication with **magnesium sulphate.**
- **Ampicillin**,**gentamicin**and  **metronidazole**for infections.
- **Diazepam** for pain relief.
- **Phenobarbital** for the newborn in case of convulsions.
- **Adrenaline** for the newborn in case of cardiac arrest.

## Supplies:

- Towels
- IV-equipment + IV fluids
- Blood pressure monitor
- Adult and newborn ambu bag with appropriate mask sizes
- Suction bulb
- Syringes of different sizes
- Examination and sterile gloves

### During transportion

![Image](6088042776584320bc945b2e902f68c4)

Observe vital signs and level of consciousness.

In the case of excessive vaginal bleeding, apply bimanual compression of the uterus.

If the bleeding does not stop apply aortic compression (can be maintained up to 6 hours).

If the woman’s condition permits:

Prevent hypoglycemia and hypothermia of the newborn.

Record all IV fluids, medications given, time of administration and the woman's and newborn's condition.
