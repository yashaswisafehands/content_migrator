# Chapter: Abortion counselling

This checklist can be used to provide abortion counselling.

![Image](36cb4a2381f84120a1701ec69d58059e)

## Guiding principles of respectful abortion care:

- Treat all women with dignity and respect
- Maintain confidentiality and privacy
- Allow a companion of her own choice
- Remain non-judgmental and impartial
- Do not discriminate
- Support every woman’s right to information and informed autonomous decision making

## Welcome the woman and explain what will happen during her visit:

1. Reassure the woman that everything discussed will be confidential. 
2. Let her know that she can ask questions at any point 
3. Explain the different abortion methods
4. Explain what she is likely to experience using the different methods
5. Explain what will be done before and after each procedure
6. Inform of the risks and complications of the different methods
7. Obtain consent by enabling informed choice 
8. Inform that, before leaving, she will be given her chosen contraceptive method (if she wants one), medication, supplies, information and contact details so that she can call if she has any questions, worries or experience any warning signs.

**Provide information on the abortion options**that are suitable for the women based on their medical history, gestational age and**availability at your facility**.

**If the requested method is not available at your facility or her medical history requires a higher-level facility, refer the women accordingly.**

## 1. Explain the different types of abortion methods:

**Medical Abortion:**

- Medical abortion is where pills are taken over a 2-3 day period, and the pregnancy will come out with bleeding that resembles a heavy period. 
- The medicines used for medical abortion are either a combination of mifepristone (or letrozole) and misoprostol, or misoprostol alone. If the pregnancy is less than 12 weeks, both methods are very effective. This can be done at home. Find more information about this in the treatment regime section for medical abortion.
- Depending on the gestation, 95 out of 100 women will have a successful abortion when using both mifepristone and misoprostol. If misoprostol is used alone, 75-90 out of 100 abortions will succeed. The dosage of medicines used depends on the gestation – see the drug list for details. 
- Bleeding from medical abortion can continue for up to 2 weeks afterwards, but it should gradually lessen in volume.

**Surgical Abortion:**

- Surgical Abortion is a procedure where a small plastic tube is inserted in the uterus and the pregnancy is removed through suction using syringe. 
- 98 to 99 out of 100 women will have a successful procedure. 
- The type of surgery and pain control will depend on how far the pregnancy has progressed.

## 2. Decision making and informed consent

- Ask the women if she has decided what service she would prefer and if she has further questions. 
- When you are confident that she understands the information, obtain verbal consent for the procedure and pain medication and ask her to sign the relevant consent form. 
- Document the care as necessary, in compliance with relevant guidelines.

## 3. Explain follow-up

- Inform the woman about what to expect afterwards, potential side-effects, who to contact and what to do in case of complications. See action cards for information on warning signs. 
- Follow-up is not routinely needed if the woman no longer has symptoms of continuing pregnancy, if she feels fine and there are no complications or warning sings. 
- If the woman is self-administering abortion medication at home, you should give her comprehensive information about what to expect. She should also be informed about how to know and check that the abortion is complete. 
- Let her know that fertility can return as early as 7 days after abortion, which can put her at risk falling pregnant again unless an effective contraceptive method is used. See contraceptive methods for more information. 
- Offer contraception to all women. However, on the day of the abortion, some women may not feel ready to decide or may not even want to start using contraception.
