# Chapter: When to discharge from NBSU:

1. Baby is maintaining maintaining normal body temperature (in room temperature/ when cared for by the mother)
2. Baby not requiring IV fluids/ medications
3. Baby is accepting breastfeeds/ assisted feeds well and gaining weight for 3 consecutive days
4. IV antibiotic therapy has been completed
5. Baby admitted for phototherapy no more requires it.
6. Mother has been counselled for danger signs*, assisted feeding & KMC if required, and plan for follow up

# Chapter: Preparation for Discharge

1. Prepare discharge summary. The birth weight, discharge weight, length and head circumference should be mentioned in the discharge summary. Suggested format provided in annexure.
2. Also inform ASHAs/ANM about the discharge and home based follow up
3. Baby should have received the following vaccines- BCG, zero dose OPV (can be given till 14th day of life) and birth dose of hepatitis B (within 24 hours of birth). There is no lower weight limit for vaccinating a newborn.
4. Advice screening for preterm babies at higher centres for screening (1. ROP for all babies < 34 weeks or <1750 gms; within first month of life; 2. BERA (Brainstem evoked response audiometry) at 40 weeks corrected age for cases requiring SNCU admission)

While explaining the discharge instruction to mother:

1. Use words that she understands
2. Use teaching aids that are familiar to her
3. Give feedback when she practices. Praise what was done well and make corrections if necessary
4. Allow more practice, if needed
5. Encourage the parent/mother to ask questions and answer all her questions.
6. **Advise mother to give home care : Breastfeed infant exclusively, keep infant warm, apply nothing to cord, handwashing & danger signs.**
