# Chapter: Indications and Usage

Diazepam is a benzodiazepine.

Diazepam is used to treat anxiety, agitation and convulsions.

Can also be used as analgesia before a painful procedure.

# Dosage and Administration

##

Diazepam 0.5 mg/kg rectally once.

If convulsions persist after 10 minutes, repeat: 

Diazepam 0.5 mg/kg rectally

If convulsions persist after an additional 10 minutes, administer:

Diazepam 0.4 mg/kg IV maximum once.

## Adults:

Before procedure:

10 mg IV OR

10-20 mg IM OR

20 mg rectally OR

**Second dose:**

10 mg IV/IM/ rectally

**For convulsions:**

If convulsions occur in early pregnancy or if Magnesium Sulphate toxicity occurs or Magnesium Sulphate is not available.

**Loading dose IV:**

Give Diazepam 10 mg IV slowly over 2 minutes.

If convulsions recur, repeat 10mg.

**Maintenace dose:**

Give Diazepam 40 mg in 500 ml IV fluids (Normal Saline or Ringer’s Lactate) titrated over 6-8 hours to keep the woman sedated but arousable.

Stop the maintenance dose if breathing is affected.

Assist ventilation, if necessary, with mask and bag.

Do not give more than 100 mg in 24 hours.

If IV access is not possible (e.g., during convulsions), give Diazepam rectally.

**Loading dose rectally:**

Diazepam 20 mg (4 ml) by administering with a 10 ml syringe (or urinary catheter):

Remove the needle, lubricate the barrel and insert half of the syringe into the rectum.

Administer the contents and leave the syringe in place, holding the buttocks together for 10 minutes to prevent expulsion of the drug.

If convulsions recur, repeat diazepam 10 mg.

**Maintenance dose:**

Administer diazepam 10 mg (2 ml) hourly during transportation.

# Contraindications

Alcohol intoxication.

Severe respiratory or liver illness

Hypersensitivity to diazepam

# Adverse reactions

Common adverse reactions are sedation, fatigue, confusion, irritability, headache, nausea, drowsiness and constipation.

Uncommon reactions are muscle weakness, coordination difficulties, headache, concentration difficulty and dizziness.

Rare reactions are respiratory depression, aggression, hallucinations, psychosis, and allergic reactions.

Diazepam should not be used long term as tolerance and addiction occurs commonly.
