# Chapter: The partograph:

The partograph is a graphical record that helps assess the progress of labour. It aids in decision-making when abnormalities are detected.

![Image](56ae188d97ac4cb18ca7e380c076c475)

All observations and findings should be recorded on the partograph when the woman has cervical dilatation of >5 cm.

## Parts of the partgraph:

1. Identification 
2. Fetal status
3. Labour Progress
4. Medication              
5. Maternal condition

---

# Chapter: Identification

The partograph starts with the identification information of the mother. This section includes:

- Name
- Gravida 
- Para 
- Hospital No. 
- Date of admission 
- Time of admission 
- Ruptured membranes (Intact (I), Ruptured (R)) 
- Hour of ruptured membranes

# Chapter: Fetal status

On the fetal status section, record fetal indicators such as:

## Fetal Heart Rate (FHR):

![Image](fd91343be07c4ecf835507997b142132)

- Count the FHR immediately after a contraction for 1 min. 
- Monitor every 30 min for low-risk pregnancy, and every 15 min for high-risk pregnancy in first stage of labour. 
- If available, continuous electronic FHR monitoring is preferred to monitor high-risk pregnancy. 
- Monitor every 5 minutes in second stage of labour.

## Amniotic Fluid:

Observe its color, odor, and quantity.

- Clear fluid

**Grading of meconium**

- **Grade I:** Good volume of fluid, lightly meconium-stained.
- **Grade II:** Reasonable volume of fluid with a heavy suspension of meconium.
- **Grade III:** Thick meconium

**NOTE:** A newly appearing meconium is an alarming sign.

## Molding:

Grading of fetal molding

- **No molding:** The fetal cranial bones are separated along the suture lines. 
- **Grade I:** The fetal cranial bones are touching each other along the suture lines. 
- **Grade II:** The fetal cranial bones are overlapping but can be separated.  
- **Grade III:** The fetal cranial bones are overlapping & are not separable

# Chapter: Labour progress

Vaginal examination is done to evaluate cervical dilatation, station, fetal position, fetal presentation, pelvic adequacy, the status of liquid, molding, and caput.

Vaginal examination should be performed every 4 hours. Always ask for consent before doing any examination.

It should be repeated after spontaneous rupture of membranes in case of abnormal FHR or to confirm the second stage of labor, if needed.

## Cervical Dilatation

![Image](c886733b66214929968015a5774d2394)

- The central feature of the partograph is the graph used to record cervical dilation. It starts by marking **5 cm of dilation**. 
- The dilatation of the cervix is plotted with an “X” on the alert line when starting the partograph.  
- Crossing the alert line calls for a thorough re-evaluation of maternal and fetal conditions.

See Prolonged Labour module or refer the woman to higher facility.

## Descent of the Fetal Head

Dilatation of the cervix should be accompanied by the descent of the fetal head, which is plotted on the same section of the partograph but using O as the symbol.

![Image](b25a5ac960d7451abcb992beecadce17)

- When the fetal head is at the same level as the ischial spines, this is called station 0.
- If the head is higher up the birth canal than the ischial spines, the station is given a negative number.  
- If the fetal head is lower down the birth canal than the ischial spines, the station is given a positive number.

# Chapter: Maternal condition

## This section is for information about maternal indicators such as:

- **Vital signs:** Pulse rate every 30 min. Blood pressure every 2 hours or more frequently if indicated.
- **Temperature**: Every 4 hours or more frequently if indicated. 
- **Urine output:** Monitor urine output, and test for ketones and protein.
