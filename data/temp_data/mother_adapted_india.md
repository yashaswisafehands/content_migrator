# Chapter: Routine postnatal assessments (up to 6 weeks)

![Image](97007b7518f6415190168968ba12b41f)

## Ask

- When and where did you deliver?
- Who conducted the delivery? 
- Have you had any of the following since delivery: excessive vaginal bleeding (number of pads soaked), loss of consciousness or convulsions, leg pain, abdominal pain, fever, urinary issues (dribbling or retention), or any breast tenderness.
- Have you started having sex with your partner since delivery? 
- Are you breastfeeding the baby?
- Have you any complaints about any breast tenderness, lumps?
- Have you decided on any contraception? 
- Have you started taking your regular diet?
- Ask if she feels sad or cries easily ?
- Do you have any other concerns?
- Ask about significant symptoms (see Action Card on "Common symptoms and treatment" and "Postnatal complications")

## Check record

- Any complications during delivery?
- Receiving any treatments?
- HIV status

## Assess

- Vital signs: Check blood pressure, pulse, respiratory rate, temperature.
- Abdominal Examination: Check uterine tone. 
- Vulva and perineum: Look for tears, swelling, discharge or pus, haemorrhoids, or haematoma.
- Bleeding and lochia: Quantity, colour, and odour.
- Pallor: Look for conjunctival and palmar pallor or other signs of anaemia.
- Breast: Look for any lumps or tenderness, nipples and observe breastfeeding
- Healing of sutured lacerations or episiotomy if any and advice on care.

![Image](868d64ad6ed44d80aa605981cfb99577)

Document on the mother’s maternal record/ Mother and child protection card.

# Chapter: Postnatal maternal counselling

Ensure that the woman stays at the health facility for at least 48 hours after delivery, as most complications in mother and baby occur during this time. 

Provide individualized, culturally appropriate counselling to the woman and her family before discharge from the facility and at each visit. Inform about the next visit at the end of the counselling session.

![Image](d5b1a0d503df4c9ab28b73a1a974194b)

**Instruct the woman on:**

- Danger signs and when to seek care.
- Postnatal care and hygiene: wash the perineum daily, change pads every 4-6 hours or more frequently if heavy bleeding or lochia is present, perform hand hygiene after using the toilet, also before and after handling the newborn)
- Postnatal nutrition and hydration (recommend a balanced diet with healthy food, encourage hydration, recommend one tablet of Iron Folic Acid for 6 months, if woman is anaemic, 2 tablets of IFA should be taken , and recommend against the use of tobacco, smoking, alcohol and drugs).  
- Exercise and limit time spent being sedentary. 
- Breastfeeding (exclusive breastfeeding for six months)
- Safe sexual practices (avoid sexual intercourse until the perineal wound is healed, consider postpartum contraception)
- Take proper rest and sleep. Sleep under bed net in malaria endemic areas.

# Chapter: Common postnatal symptoms and treatment

## Perineal pain

- Examine the perineum to assess for normal healing, signs of infection, haematoma, haemorrhoids  and breakdown of the wound.
- Suggest local cooling with cold pads or cold sitz baths. Avoid direct contact with ice. 
- Recommend oral paracetamol for pain.
- Inform about perineal hygiene.
- If passing urine is painful, encourage hydration and to pour water when urinating to help dilute urine.

![Image](f6327670df434dba93c5dfab0527fb3a)

## Uterine cramping

- It is normal for women to experience uterine cramping as the uterus contracts
- Women may experience more cramping while breastfeeding
- Recommend  Ibuprofen  and paracetamol for uterine cramping

![Image](03a57ec1621148de934b641c31bec030)

## Constipation

- Encourage a healthy balanced diet with adequate intake of water and high-fibre foods (fruit, vegetables, nuts, wholegrains).
- Teach toileting habits like responding to the urge to have a bowel movement and to take the time to complete emptying.
- Encourage low impact physical activity, unless contraindicated.
- Address the underlying factors like perineal pain, altered dietary intake, etc.
- Do not recommend the routine use of laxatives. 
- Contact health facility if the condition is not relieved after these actions.

![Image](b56419a50dad4df9babbccaf3dbfe602)

## Headaches

- Assess for symptoms of infection, anaemia, blood clots and pre-eclampsia
- Encourage hydration, balanced diet and sleep

## Postpartum blues

- Assure the woman that this is a common condition.
- Listen to her concerns. Give emotional encouragement and support
- Counsel partner and family to help the woman
- If symptoms persist beyond 2-3 weeks, screen for postpartum depression and refer for specialist care (see the perinatal mental health module).

# Chapter: Postnatal complications

If any of the following symptoms are identified, offer immediate referral or treatment according to your local pathways and ensure appropriate information and investigations (See individual modules for treatment of postnatal complications).

## Infections

**Uterine infection:**

- Fever > 38 °C, shivering, abdominal pain and tenderness, or foul-smelling vaginal discharge.

**Urinary tract infection:**

- Fever > 38 °C, burning on urination, flank pain. Give antibiotics encourage fluid intake and refer to higher centre.

## Postpartum haemorrhage

- Sudden and excessive, or persistent and increased blood loss, faintness, dizziness, or palpitations. 
- Blood loss of more than 500 millilitres after vaginal birth and more than 1000 millilitres after Cesarean section, or any loss which deteriorates maternal condition.

![Image](fac32f228dc442a1bcd512eea2920468)

## Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia

- Pre-Eclampsia- can be mild or severe. In severe pre-eclampsia danger symptoms like severe headache, blurring, epigastric pain, breathing difficulty or  new onset end organ dysfunction symptoms might be present.
- Blood pressure- diastolic- ≥ 90 mmHg on 2 readings or ≥110 mmHg in one reading. 
- Eclampsia - Pregnancy with convulsion and hypertension.

## Thromboembolism

- One-sided calf pain, redness or swelling of calves, shortness of breath, or chest pain.

![Image](ee8c915b4b124d3ea59713f8d1f9f196)

## Anaemia

- Haemoglobin – less than 11g/dL, palmar or conjunctival pallor, weakness, giddiness, breathlessness etc.
- Give double dose of iron tablet – 2 IFA tablets per day till Hb becomes normal , thereafter continue 1IFA per day till 6 weeks  postpartum.  
- Refer to higher centre immediately if severe anaemia – Hb <7 g/dL, respiration > 30breaths/min, tires easily, breathlessness at rest.

![Image](1a000f35723843459e9378eeab603470)

## Postpartum depression

- Inappropriate guilt or negative, persistent sad or anxious mood
- Irritability 
- Low interest in activities that used to be enjoyable
- Difficulty in doing usual work
- Negative or hopeless feelings about herself or the newborn
- Multiple symptoms such as aches, pains and palpitations without a clear physical cause.

# Chapter: Post partum contraception

- Pregnancies within 24 months of a previous birth are linked to higher risks such as miscarriage, premature labor, low birth weight, and maternal complications.
- Provide evidence based comprehensive information and services to all women during postnatal care to ensure informed choices about postpartum contraception.
- Mode of birth does not restrict the woman’s contraceptive choices. 
- Know the breastfeeding status and the age of the newborn. 
- Explain that she can become pregnant as soon as 4 weeks after delivery if she has sex and is not exclusively breastfeeding.
- Ask about the plans of having more children and explain that waiting at least 2 years before trying for the next pregnancy is beneficial for the mother and the newborn.

![Image](b0effd70648f4fdaa2d3652cc75ac60f)

For more information about counselling on modern contraception and the different  contraceptive methods available - go to the Modern contraception module.

## Options for breastfeeding women

**Immediate postpartum**

- Condoms
- Postpartum Intrauterine contraceptive device (PPIUCD)- within 48 hours
- Lactational Amenorrhoea Method(LAM)(till 6 months) 
- Centchroman
- Progestin- only pills    
- Implants 
- Female sterilization(within 7 days of delivery)
- Male sterilization

**After 6 weeks postpartum**

- All immediate postpartum contraception methods 
- Interval IUCD
- Injection Medroxyprogestrone Acetate (MPA)

**After 6 months postpartum**

- All immediate postpartum contraception methods, except LAM method
- Combined oral contraceptives (COCs)

## Options for non-breastfeeding women

**Immediate postpartum**

- Condoms 
- Progestin- only pills 
- Injection Medroxyprogestrone Acetate (MPA)
- Implants
- Female sterilization (within 7 days of delivery)
- Male sterilization  
- Centchroman
- IUCD (within 48 hours )

**After 3 weeks**

- All immediate postpartum contraception methods and interval IUCD 
- Combined oral contraceptives (women with no risk of venous thrombus embolism. If risk is present, delay up to 6 weeks)
- **For information on emergency contraception - see the Modern contraception module.**
