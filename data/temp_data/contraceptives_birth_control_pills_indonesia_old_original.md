# Chapter: Preamble

Birth control pills come in different types.

The most common is the combination pill that has both estrogen and gestagen.

Another less effective birth control pill is the gestagen only that must be taken every day and in principle works as the implant or injectable contraceptive and has the same contraindications and adverse effects.

Below will be described the combination pill.

## Indications and Usage

Combination pills are indicated as contraception.

It can also be used to regulate the menstrual cycle, to treat acne, painful menstruation and heavy menstrual bleeding.

Remember that only condom protects against sexually transmitted diseases!

## Dosage and Administration

When starting birth control pills the first tablet is taken on the first day of bleeding in the menstrual cycle.

One tablet is taken every day for 21 days (3 weeks) approximately at the same time of the day.
The following 7 days no tablets are taken, and the menstrual bleeding will occur.

If a tablet is forgotten you should continue taking the birth control pills but use a condom at intercourse for the rest of the menstrual period.

## Contraindications

Present or previous thromboembolism, heart disease, migraine with focal neurological symptoms, diabetes with vascular complications, hypertension with diastolic blood pressure above 160 mm Hg, unexplained vaginal bleeding, jaundice in relation to pregnancy or previous use of birth control pills, breast cancer.

## Adverse Reactions

Common adverse reactions are nausea, diarrhoea, headache, menstrual pain, spot bleeding, irregular bleeding, no menstrual bleeding, weight gain, abdominal pain, constipation, breast tension, muscle pain, back pain, acne, skin rash, vaginal discharge, and urine tract infections.

Uncommon adverse reactions are arterial or venous thromboembolisms, dyspnoea, hypertension, anxiety, fainting, hair loss, visual disturbances and dryness of the eyes.

Rare adverse reactions are Pancreatitis, hepatitis, tachycardia, and breast cysts.
