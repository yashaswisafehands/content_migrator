# Chapter: Decontamination and Cleaning of instruments

1. Wear appropriate PPE  to protect from splashes and disassemble all items. 
2. All used instruments should be decontaminated in 0.5% sodium hypochlorite solution for 10 minutes, then it should be cleaned after use by placing them in detergent solution.
3. If immediately cleaning is not possible , place the instruments in a bucket and cover with a wet towel until cleaning is possible.
4. Clean instruments with a brush in the detergent solution to minimise splashing. Do not use bar soap for cleaning items.
5. Tools used for cleaning such as brush should be cleaned and disinfected after use.
6. Rinse instruments with clean, running water and air-dry before sterilization or high-level disinfection. 
7. All instruments should be individually inspected and visibly clean. Repeat cleaning if any instrument is not clean.

# Chapter: Storage of instruments

Storage of instruments and equipment is important to maintain its sterility or disinfection 
The risk of contamination is high when sterile items are in a sterilized container that is repeatedly opened and closed each time an item is removed.

If an item is contaminated it must be re-sterilized.

Therefore, divide and sterilize items for each procedure. Either double wrap them in nonwoven fabric or place them in a small, covered, dry, sterile container.

Sterilized items should be stored in a clean, dry environment and protected from any damage.

Sterile instruments should be stored in sterile trays without exposure and may be used for up to 7 days. Resterilize after 7th day.

Once the lid is opened, the tray should be used within 24 hours. 

Instruments processed with wet methods (boil/steam) are to be re-sterilized after 24 hours, if unused.

While handling instruments, use sterile cheatle forceps to take out autoclaved pads and linens from drums.

The cheatle forceps should be stored in a sterile metal jar and autoclaved or high-level disinfected every 24 hours.

Note: Do not store instruments or other items such as scalpel blades and suture needles in solution; always store them dry.

# Chapter: Housekeeping

Labour tables and surfaces should be disinfected with aldehyde preparations after each delivery, and floors and sinks cleaned with detergent and water/hospital grade phenyl.

Disinfect the facility twice daily: clean floors and sinks with detergent or hospital-grade phenyl. Also, wipe labour tables, surfaces, and furniture with 2% aldehyde solution, and disinfect toilets appropriately. 

Once a week, walls, roofs, floors, furniture, and fixtures should be thoroughly cleaned and washed.

![Image](96ba4d5a7b2548759dbb3a7c7e3dbfdc)

Maintain strict infection control in the labour room by restricting entry and external footwear. Ensure health care provider should wear PPE, at all times and managing biomedical waste as per guidelines.

Clean even if there was no birth on the delivery bed. 

Spread of germs can be prevented with simple hygiene procedures when you are persistent and disciplined
