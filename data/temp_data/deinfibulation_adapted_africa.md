# Chapter: Indications

A physical barrier caused by FGM (remember that scarred tissue does not stretch) OR

Impossible to perform vaginal examination OR

The urethra cannot be visualized.

# Chapter: Practical Information

Perform deinfibulation at antenatal care or at birth.

1.	Perform hand hygiene and wear sterile gloves.

2.	Clean the vulva area with antiseptic.

3.	Start by palpating the scar tissue to identify urethra

4.	Gently introduce a dilator and lift the scar tissues from the underlying tissue

5.	Give local analgesia into the area where the cut will be made

6.	With scissors incise in the mid-line to expose the urethral opening:

a)	If the clitoris is not intact and there is scar tissue from cutting the clitoris, do **not** incise beyond the urethra as the scar tissue may cause haemorrhage.

b)	If the clitoris is intact, perform the opening just above the clitoris.

c)	Be careful **not** to damage the urethra or the clitoris.

7.	After birth suture the raw edges from deinfibulation. Suture each raw edge with interrupted sutures to secure hemostasis and prevent the raw edges from adhering:

8.	Give oral analgesics after deinfibulation.
