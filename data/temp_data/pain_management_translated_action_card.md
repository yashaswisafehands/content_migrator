# Chapter: Pain management

To ensure that oral pain relief is effective, administer it 30-45 minutes prior to the procedure.

**Non-pharmacological methods:**

- Respectful, non-judgmental communication 
- Verbal support and reassurance
- Thorough explanation of procedure
- Presence of a support person 
- Hot water-bottle and heating pad 
- Deep and controlled breathing

![Image](ba5aa5e1987d4020a35133645c88e83b)

**Pharmacological methods:**

- NSAIDs (e.g. Ibuprofen 400-800mg)
- Anxiolytics/sedatives (e.g. Diazepam 5-10mg)

## Surgical abortion

For pain management for surgical abortion (additional to NSAIDS) the use of a paracervical block is recommended. If available, offer this in combination with conscious sedation.

- Local anesthetics (paracervical block w. lidocaine 10-20 ml of 0,5 - 1%)

**Example of paracervical block:**

![Image](a6acb69dc5024c9db88664e092ec12c5)

- Inject 1-2 ml of anaesthetic at the cervical site where the tenaculum will be placed (either at 12 o’ clock or 6 o’clock). 
- Stabilize the cervix with the tenaculum at the anaesthetized site.
- Use slight traction to move the cervix and locate the transition of smooth cervical epithelium to vaginal tissue, which delineates the placement for additional injections. 
- Slowly inject lidocaine 2-5 ml into a depth of 1,5-3 cm at 2-4 points at the cervical/vaginal junction (2 and 10 o’clock, and/or 4 and 8 o’clock).
- Move the needle while injecting OR aspirate before injection to avoid intravascular injection. 
- The maximum dose of lidocaine in a paracervical block is 4,5 mg/kg/dose or generally 200-300 mg (approximately 20 ml of 1% or 40 ml of 0,5%).

## Medical abortion > 12 weeks gestation

In addition to NSAIDs offer at least one or more of following:

- Diazepam or Morphine, intramuscular (IM) or intravenous (IV). 
- Epidural anesthesia, if available.
