# Chapter: Nutrition and lifestyle

### Nutrition and lifestyle

![Image](2e39ecc4ddc346ca871a7879c2d6d73c)

**Counsel the pregnant women on:**

- Healthy diet containing adequate energy, protein, vitamins, calcium and minerals, obtained through the consumption of a variety of foods, including milk and dairy products, green leafy and other vegetables, egg, meat, fish, beans, nuts, whole grains, pulses, ragi, jaggerry and fruit. 
- Recommend healthy eating and exercise to prevent excessive weight gain during pregnancy.
- Pregnant women who are undernourished, a previous childbirth within 2 years, multiple pregnancy and HIV positive should take more calories and protein.
- Include more fibre in the diet to avoid constipation.
- Use only iodised or double fortified salt.
- Talk about food taboos and address them if any.
- Avoid alcohol and tobacco in any form.
- Reduce daily caffeine intake to reduce the risk of pregnancy loss and low birthweight. 
- Rest adequately and avoid lifting heavy objects.
- Do at least 30 minutes of energetic walking  or light exercise a day 
- Personal hygiene should be maintained.
- Do not take medications unless prescribed at a health centre/ hospital

- Safer sex and use of condoms to prevent STI's.

- Emphasize importance of family support during pregnancy.

![Image](8f0217f894574f888eaa768ffa249d8a)

**Weight gain during pregnancy should be as per the pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI)**

- Underweight (BMI < 18.5 kg/m2) - 12.5 to 18 kg weight gain
- Normal weight (BMI 18.5 - 24.9 kg/m2) - 11.5 to 16 kg weight gain
- Overweight (BMI 25 – 29.9 kg/m2) - 7 to 11.5 kg weight gain
- Obese women (BMI >30 kg/m2) - 5 to 9 kg weight gain.

# Chapter: Nutritional supplements 

## Counsel on nutritional supplements:

## Folic Acid

- Once pregnancy is confirmed:  folic acid 400 mcg once a day during 1st trimester to prevent  neural tube defects. 
- See ‘Folic Acid in’ the Drug List

### Iron and Folic Acid

![Image](3bbb9c8a085b4c13a81becf4cb15671b)

- Daily oral Iron and Folic Acid supplementation with 60 mg of elemental Iron and 500 mcg (0.5mg) of Folic Acid is recommended to prevent maternal anaemia, low birth weight, puerperal sepsis, preterm birth and neural tube defects.

**Dosage**: 30 mg to 60 mg of elemental iron and 400 mcg (0.4 mg) of folic acid

See ‘Iron and Folic Acid Supplements’ in the Drug List

### Calcium

![Image](bffaf1dceccb4ede98e98d346efc7260)

- Provide one tablet, containing 500 mg Calcium and 250 international units of Vitamin D3, twice a day for at least 6 months, starting from 14 weeks of pregnancy to reduce the risk of pre-eclampsia.

**Dosage**: 1.5–2.0 g oral elemental calcium

See ‘Calcium supplementation’ in the Drug List

![Image](00602f3f861b406782c5f890897c9cb8)

Vitamin A supplementation is recommended to prevent night blindness in areas where vitamin A deficiency is a severe public health problem, if:

> 5% of women in a population have a history of night blindness in their most recent pregnancy in the previous 3–5 years that ended in a live birth, OR 
> 20% of pregnant women have a serum retinol level < 0.70 µmol/L.

**Dosage:**10 000 IU vitamin A per day, or 25 000 IU per week

See ‘Vitamin A’ in the Drug List
