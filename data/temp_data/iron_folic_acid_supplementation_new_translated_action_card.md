# Chapter: Indications and Usage

Routine antenatal care: Recommended for all pregnant women. Folic acid should be commenced as early as possible (ideally before conception) to prevent neural tube defects.

- Reduces maternal anaemia and iron deficiency. 
- Lowers the risk of low birth weight and neural tube defects.
- Decreases the likelihood of puerperal sepsis.

# Dosage and Administration

**Routine antenatal supplementation:**

- Elemental iron 30-60 mg orally AND folic acid 0.4 mg orally daily.

**Poor toleration of daily iron and settings with antenatal anaemia prevalence of < 20%:**

- Elemental iron 120 mg orally, AND folic acid 2.8 mg orally weekly.

**Settings with antenatal anaemia prevalence of ≥ 40%:**

- Elemental iron 60 mg orally, AND folic acid 0.4 mg orally daily.

**Diagnosed antenatal anaemia (<110g/L):**

- Elemental iron 120 mg orally, AND folic acid 0.4 mg orally daily until haemoglobin returns to normal (Hb 110 g/L or higher), then resume the standard antenatal dose.

# Contradictions

## Folic acid

Doses of folic acid above 0.4 mg daily should not be given without vitamin B12, unless vitamin B12 deficiency can be ruled out.

## Iron:

- Hypersensitivity
- Iron metabolism disorders causing increased iron storage
- Overload of iron in the blood
- Haemolytic anaemia
- Stomach ulcers and gastritis

# Considerations

Iron and calcium supplements should preferably be administered several hours apart rather than concomitantly as negative interactions between these micronutrients may occur.

The equivalent of elemental iron 120 mg is:

Ferrous sulphate heptahydrate 600 mg, OR
Ferrous fumarate 360 mg, OR
Ferrous gluconate 1000 mg

In malaria-endemic areas, provision of IFA supplements should be implemented in conjunction with measures to prevent, diagnose and treat malaria.
