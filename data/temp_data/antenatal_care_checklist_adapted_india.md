# Chapter: Overview

### Overview

Regular antenatal care, throughout the antenatal period improves health outcomes for both women and newborns.

![Image](0c11f4f03f204967846c108bc3953a1c)

A minimum of 4 antenatal visits are recommended to prevent and identify complications and take appropriate action.

One visit during 2nd or 3rd trimester is recommended under Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA), where a pregnant woman should be checked by a doctor on the 9th day of the month.

If a pregnant woman is categorized to have a High-Risk Pregnancy (HRP), ensure at least 3 additional ANC visits by a Doctor or Obstetrician.

# Chapter: 1st Trimester: Contact 1 (above 12 weeks)

### 1st Trimester: 1st ANC Visit (Within 12 weeks)

**Take her history**

- Confirm the pregnancy, record the date of LMP and calculate EDD. 
- Assess for significant symptoms. 
- Take full medical, psychosocial, and family medical history 
- Take obstetric history - Number and details of previous pregnancies/live births/miscarriages/abortions, previous obstetric complications (pre-eclampsia, haemorrhage etc), obstetric surgeries and history of blood transfusion. 
- Ask for any other diseases like diabetes, chronic hypertension, heart, kidney, or  thyroid dysfunction, convulsions, asthma or any autoimmune disease. 
- Ask about vaccination status, recent medications, and any drug allergies.
- Ask about exposure to tobacco, second-hand smoke, use of alcohol or other substances. 
- In privacy, ask if the woman has experienced gender-based violence or intimate partner violence (See Action Card on Violence against women)  
- Give filled up MCP card, and safe motherhood booklet to the women.

![Image](e4dd1c926c0a481584f5f6fbfe18ef63)

**Examine**

- Weight and height 
- Blood pressure 
- Pulse rate
- Pallor
- Oedema
- Jaundice
- Nutritional status

**Laboratory investigations**

<color style="blue">Urine for</color>

- Haemoglobin
- Urine for albumin and sugar
- Blood group and Rh status 
- HIV 
- Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) for Syphilis  
- Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT)
- Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) for HBsAg
- Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) for Malaria if the woman has fever

![Image](60e10db826de4c998907e3031d779d6d)

<color style="blue">Blood for</color>

- Haemoglobin
- Blood group and Rh status
- HIV
- Syphilis 
- Tuberculosis (if relevant)

![Image](722f1968f7c844b981d9b54d93b96ef8)

**Treat/Refer**
Follow local guidelines for referral and management of any abnormal findings.

**Preventative measures**

- Folic acid
- Tetanus and diphtheria vaccination 
- Insecticide treated bed nets (ITN) for malaria prevention

![Image](3bbb9c8a085b4c13a81becf4cb15671b)

<color style="blue">Context specific:</color>

- Vitamin A
- Calcium  
- Limit caffeine to <300mg p/d.
- Malaria prevention (at 13 weeks) 
- HIV prevention

**Counsel the woman on**

- Birth Preparedness and complication readiness planEntitlement under Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakaram(JSSK)and  JananiSurakshaYojana (JSY)
- Healthy eating, being physically active and rest
- Safe Sex during pregnancy
- Domestic violence
- Avoid using tobacco, alcohol and other substances 
- Interventions for common physiological symptoms

### Summary and consolidation

- Classify the woman’s obstetric risk 
- Revisit her birth plan, emergency signs and symptoms 
- Confirm her contact details 
- Give health facility contact and next appointment details ( between 14 to 26 weeks) 
- Update the woman's antenatal records, MCP card and give them to her

See normal labour and birth module for practical procedures for calculation of EDD and gestational age.

# Chapter: 2nd Trimester: Contacts 2 and 3 (weeks 20 and 26)

### 2nd Trimester: 2nd ANC Visit (Between 14 to 26 weeks)

## Ask

- Any Symptoms or discomfort 
- Compliance to the medications / supplements
- Ask if the woman has experienced gender-based violence or intimate partner violence. . (See Action Card on Violence against women)

![Image](0fc6be68361844d5aa9723e76a71bfea)

## Examine

- Blood pressure and pulse
- Weight  
- Pallor
- Oedema
- Jaundice

<color style="blue">Assess for signs of: </color>

- Anaemia 
- Pre-eclampsia 
- Urinary tract/other infections 
- Threatened preterm labour 
- Antepartum haemorrhage 
- Miscarriage/Stillbirth 
- Gestational diabetes

![Image](fdaf8a70833849c5a62a6e8c2a0ed972)

<color style="blue">Foetal examination:</color>

- Check Foetal heart rate (week 24)
- Measure fundal height (week 24) 
- Ask about foetal movements (week 28)

![Image](13b8ada3c5204a9abf31acf9aab352e1)

## Test

<color style="blue">Urine for</color>

- Proteinuria
- Glucosuria

![Image](60e10db826de4c998907e3031d779d6d)

<color style="blue">Blood for</color>

- Haemoglobin
- Sugar (OGTT) (24-28 weeks)

![Image](722f1968f7c844b981d9b54d93b96ef8)

## Preventative measures

- Iron and folic acid 
- Calcium  
- Albendazole ( preferably within 2nd trimester) 
- Tetanus and diphtheria vaccination  2nd dose

## Counselling (reinforce previous advice)

- Birth and emergency plan /BPCR.
- Healthy eating and keeping physically active 
- Interventions for common physiological symptoms 
- Breastfeeding
- Contraception counselling

## Summary and consolidation

- Re-classify the woman’s obstetric risk 
- Revisit her birth plan, emergency signs and symptoms 
- Confirm her contact details 
- Give health facility contact and next appointment details (Between 28-34 weeks) 
- Update the woman's antenatal records, MCP card and give them to her

# Chapter: 3rd Trimester: Contacts 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 (weeks 30, 34, 36, 38 and 40)

### 3rd Trimester: Visit 3 and 4  (between weeks, 28  to 34 and 36 and term)

## Ask

- Any symptoms or discomfort 
- Compliance to the medications / supplements 
- Ask if the woman has experienced gender-based violence or intimate partner violence (See Action Card on Violence against women)

![Image](cda7796e499446da88231a8e3773259e)

## Examine

- Blood pressure 
- Weight 
- Pallor
- Oedema
- Jaundice

<color style="blue">Assess for significant symptoms of for example:  </color>

- Anaemia 
- Pre-eclampsia 
- Urinary tract/other infections 
- Threatened preterm labour 
- Antepartum haemorrhage 
- Foetal compromise /death 
- Gestational diabetes

**Foetal examination**

- Listen and count foetal heart rate
- Measure fundal height
- Ask about foetal movements
- Palpate for multiple pregnancy 
- Palpate for malposition (from week 34)

![Image](c94a3af75b914354b586e400e6846781)

![Image](3c8df14fb1824e048834e7950ddbe40a)

## Test

<color style="blue">Urine </color>

- Urine for albumin and sugar 
- Haemoglobin 
- OGTT (week between 24 to 28)

<color style="blue">Blood </color>

- Haemoglobin (week 36)

**Treat/Refer**
Follow local guidelines for referral and management of any abnormal findings

## Preventative measures

- Iron and folic acid  
- Calcium

<color style="blue">Context specific</color>

- Vitamin A 
- Calcium 
- Limit caffeine to <300mg p/d
- Malaria prevention
- Anti-D prophylaxis for Rh-negative women (week 34)

## Health education, advice, and counselling (reinforce previous advice)

- Birth preparedness and complication readiness
- Observe for foetal movements.
- Infant feeding 
- Postpartum/postnatal care 
- Birth spacing and contraception 
- Adequate rest, healthy eating and keeping physically active. 
- Tobacco usage and exposure, and use of alcohol and other substances 
- Interventions for common physiological symptoms

### Summary and finalising contact

- Re-classify the woman’s obstetric risk 
- Revisit her birth plan, emergency signs and symptoms 
- Confirm her contact details 
- Give health facility contact and next appointment details (between 36 and 40 weeks) 
- Update the woman's antenatal records, MCP card and give them to her
