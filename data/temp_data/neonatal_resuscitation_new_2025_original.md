# Chapter: Neonatal resuscitation

## The golden minute

By  the end of their first minute of life, every newborn should either be:

- Breathing on their own - OR
- Ventilated with a bag and a mask.

## Immediate steps after birth:

- Dry thoroughly with a clean towel. Replace wet towels and cover to keep warm.
- Stimulate breathing by rubbing the newborn's back firmly.
- Clear airway if needed.

## If the newborn is:

- Not crying or breathing
- Apeears limp
- Has bluish or pale colour

## Initial resucitation steps

> CALL FOR HELP

Alert your team immediately to intiate neonatal resuscitation support.

> Airway and Breathing

- If thick meconium or secretions are obstructing the airway, suction their mouth and nose before ventilating. 
- Postion the head in a "sniffing" position.
- Position mask so it covers their mouth and nose, use the “C” and “E” grip. 
- Give ventilation breaths at a rate of approximately 40-60 breaths/ minute.  
- Observe that their chest is rising with each breath (if not, reassess the head and mask position. Consider usings a jaw thrust to open the airway). 
- Continue ventilation breaths while reassessing regularly.

## Heart rate and breathing assessment:

Assess every 30 seconds.

[TableStart]
| Heart rate                               | Recommended action                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| < 60 bpm after 1 minute of ventilation   | Start chest compressions if 2+ providers available. 3 compressions: 1 breath rhythm. |
| < 60 bpm (ongoing)                       | Continue ventilation with compressions. Consider adrenaline.                         |
| 60-100 bpm or irregular breathing        | Continue ventilation breaths.                                                        |
| > 100 bpm, breathing well, normal colour | Stop ventilation. Observe every 5 minutes.                                           |
| < 100 bpm, breathing well, but bluish    | Administer oxygen via nasal catheter or nasal prongs.                                |
|                                          |                                                                                      |
|                                          |                                                                                      |
[TableEnd]

## When to stop resuscitation

If the newborn is still not breathing and:

- Has no pulse after 10 min or effective ventilation, OR
- Heart rate remains < 60 bpm for over 20 minutes.

Consider stopping resuscitation.

Explain gently to the mother, that unfortunately their newborn did not survive. Provide emotional support to the family.
