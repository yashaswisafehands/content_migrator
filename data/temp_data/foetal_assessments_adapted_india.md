# Chapter: Before performing abdominal examinations 

### Before performing abdominal examinations

![Image](f9f54c104f7b4d7e9c76559891d0493f)

- Explain procedure and gain verbal consent 
- Ensure privacy.
- Ask the woman to empty the bladder 
- Assist the woman to lie down comfortably in a supine position with legs partially flexed 
- Stand on the right side of woman while doing examinations 
- Wash hands and warm them 
- Centralize the uterus and look for scars on the abdomen.
- Ensure uterus is not contracted.

# Chapter: Listen to foetal heart rate (FHR) (from 20 weeks)

### Listen to foetal heart rate (FHR) (from 24 weeks)

![Image](aa1d9d3fcc2f4946b1161e49d73e9b1b)

- Fetal heart sound is best heard on the back of the fetus.
- Count the FHR for 1 full minute. 
- Normal FHR is 120-160 bpm.
- FHR is < 120 or > 160 bpm indicates foetal distress, refer immediately.

# Chapter: Foetal movements (FM) (from 28 weeks)

### Foetal movements (from 28 weeks)

![Image](d8051fffbdd74de7b5a448862b2b09ab)

- Ask if she can feel the foetal movements as it indicates foetal wellbeing. 
- Normally 10-12 movements should be felt in a day.
- Reduced fetal movements may indicate fetal distress.
- Women with reduced or no foetal movements require additional monitoring and referral to higher center.

# Chapter: Feel for number of foetuses (from 30 weeks)

### Foetal lie and presentation (from 32 weeks)

![Image](ff78acd2919b43f7a4913b1b8099dd85)

- Gently palpate the abdomen 
- Feel for number of foetus and identify multiple pregnancies. 
- Consider a multiple pregnancy if:

- Fundal height is more than expected.
- Three or more foetal poles are palpable. 
- Multiple limb clusters palpable.

Identify foetal:

- Lie - longitudinal, transverse or oblique 
- Presentation - cephalic, breech or shoulder 
- Position - direction the foetus is facing 
- Engagement - presenting part fixed in the pelvis or free. How much of it can you feel?

# Chapter: Feel for malpresentation (from 34 weeks)

### Feel for malpresentation (from 34 weeks)

![Image](76eee60e606c416d87b08a4f27fbd260)

![Image](28bb91c53d904969bc31ff5637c16913)

**Identify foetal:**

- **Lie:** longitudinal, transverse or oblique
- **Position:** direction the foetus is facing
- **Presentation:** cephalic, breech or shoulder
- **Engagement:** presenting part fixed in the pelvis or free. How much of it can you feel?

If the foetus is not cephalic, consult referral hospital for management, inform the woman and prepare an emergency birth plan.

![Image](2001a38096a2486882c6680a2b440861)

If the foetus is cephalic and not engaged, encourage to contact hospital immediately in the event of ruptured membranes or signs of labour.

**Refer to normal labour and birth module practical procedures for details on foetal heart rate and abdominal examination.**

# Chapter: Measure fundal height (from 24 weeks)

![Image](a209dfbd8b64416ab7f03268f740f844)

- The woman’s legs should be kept straight.
- Measure the distance in centimeter between the upper boarder of the symphysis pubis and the top of the fundus using a measuring tape.

The gestational age (in weeks) corresponds to the fundal height (in cm) after 24 weeks of gestation With a deviation of+/- 1 to 2cm

- If the fundal height is  ≥ 3 cm than the pregnancy week, it might be multiple pregnancy, large baby or polyhydramnios
- If fundal height is ≤ 3 cm compared to the pregnancy week, it might be growth restriction, transverse lie, oligohydramnios.

To estimate the gestational age by fundal height may the abdomen is divided into parts by imaginary lines. 

Keep the horizontal line passing through the umbilicus as the centre and divide the lower and upper abdomen into three parts, with two imaginary equidistant lines.

The position of fundus at the level of umbilicus corresponds to 24 weeks of gestation.
