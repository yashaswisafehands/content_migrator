# Chapter: Breastfeeding

## Steps for effective breastfeeding support

1. Encourage skin-to-skin contact between mother and newborn soon after birth.
2. Help the mother to start breastfeeding as soon as possible, within the first hour.
3. Let the newborn feed on demand, day and night.
4. Check positioning, attachment, and suckling.
5. Offer practical support with breastfeeding techniques.
6. Assist with common breastfeeding problems (e.g. pain, engorgement, poor latch).

**Support the mother to breastfeed by:**

- Letting the mother and newborn stay together day and night.
- Making sure that mothers of sick newborns can stay near them.

## Feeding cues and practices

- Help the mother recognise early hunger signs.
- The newborn should fee on demand, without restricted feeding times. 
- Let the newborn finish feeding on one breast before offering the other.

## Correct attachment

The newborn is well attached when:

- Their chin is touching the breast
- Their mouth is opened wide.
- Their bottom lip is turned outward
- More areola is visible above than below the mouth.

## Ineffective attachment

Signs of ineffective attachement:

- Baby’s mouth is not wide open 
- Only the nipple (not the areola) is in the baby’s mouth 
- Baby’s cheeks are sucked in or dimpled while sucking 
- Clicking or smacking sounds during feeding 
- Baby’s seems unsatisfied or feeds very often 
- Mother feels pain during feeding 
- Nipples look pinched or flattened after feeding 
- Breasts remain full or engorged after feeding

## Feeding recommendations

- Practice exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months. 
- Continue breastfeeding for up to 2 years or beyond.
- Give only breastmilk unless there are medical reason not to.
- Support a mother who wants to formula feed to do so safely and hygienically.
