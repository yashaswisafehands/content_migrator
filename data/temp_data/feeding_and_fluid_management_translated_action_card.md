# Chapter: Feeding and fluid management of newborns

Newborns should be fed with their mother’s own breastmilk whenever possible. Initiate breastfeeding as soon as possible—ideally within the first hour after birth—and allow the newborn to feed on demand.

- Total daily fluid volume (ml/kg/day) depends on birth weight and age. 
- The total daily volume of fluids is spread over 24 hours. 
- Fluids can be given orally, IV, or in a combination based on the newborn’s condition.
- Reference weight for recommended intake is the birth weight until it is exceeded.

## Newborn feeding and fluid management

> Remember to always follow national guidelines on newborn feeding and fluid care. 

See below for suggested management.

Feed on schedule every 3-5 hours

**Feeding guidelines for preterm and very low-birthweight (LBW):**

Mother’s own breastmilk is recommended for feeding preterm or LBW newborns, including very preterm (< 32 weeks’) or very LBW newborns (< 1.5 kg). When the mother's breastmilk is unavailable, donor milk can be considered for these newborns.

**Early feeding is essential:**Preterm and LBW, including very preterm (<32 weeks) and very LBW (<1,5 kg) newborns, should be fed as early as possible from the first day after birth:

- **If able to breastfeed:**Encourage breastfeeding as soon as possible. 
- **If unable to breastfeed:** Provide expressed mother’s breastmilk as soon as it is available.  
- **If the mother's breastmilk is unavailable:** Prioritize donor human milk wherever possible.

**For very preterm or very LBW newborns:** 
If breastfeeding is not possible and alternative feeding methods (like gastric tube or cup feeding) are needed, increase the feed volume by up to 30 ml/kg per day.

**Birthweight < 1.5 kg:**

Feed on schedule every 2 hours.

**If very LBW or unable to tolerate oral feeds:**

- Start with 10% glucose solution IV.
- After the first two days of life, change to half saline or 5% dextrose. 
- Reintroduce expressed breastmilk: Begin with small amounts, gradually increasing by 1-2 ml per feed daily. 
- If IV fluids are given, reduce the rate as the volume of oral or gastric milk feeds increases. 
- When sucking well at the breast and gaining weight reduce the cup or gastric tube feeds.

**Remember to include WHO-recommended supplementation**of iron, zinc, vitamin D, and vitamin A per national protocols for newborn care.
