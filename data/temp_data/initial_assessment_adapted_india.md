# Chapter: Initial assessment

When a labouring woman arrives at your health facility, you should perform a quick initial assessment of both the woman and the foetus.

## Examine for danger signs

Examine for danger signs such as:

- Severe headache/blurred vision
- Fever 
- Bleeding 
- Severe abdominal pain 
- Premature rupture of membranes.

![Image](432f5abaf7404f55adf941113a4beab9)

## Check vital signs

Check that vital signs are within normal range:

- Temperature: ≤ 38 °C 
- Maternal pulse: 60-110 bpm 
- Systolic BP: 90-139 mmHg 
- Diastolic BP: 60-89 mmHg

![Image](4761a435e799421189898f10787543e7)

## Look for signs of anemia and dehydration:

**Anaemia:**

- Look for paleness inside the conjunctiva/eyelids and palms

![Image](3bfb35e8d79843359ec099a1f01743b0)

**Dehydration:**

- Look for sunken eyes and dry mouth 
- Pinch the skin of her forearm to see if it goes back quickly

![Image](696eb84e0b094b7bb7d366b8a3d6bb17)

## Review medical records

Evaluate Hb, HIV, syphilis and Hepatitis B status, status of STIs, TB, GDM,  TSH and other conditions, which require special considerations during labour and birth.

## Take a complete history

- Ask about her medical history 
- Ask about past and current pregnancies
