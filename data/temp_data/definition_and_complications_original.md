### Definitions

There are four types of Female Genital Mutilations:

Type 1 - Partial or total removal of the clitoris.

Type 2 - Partial or total removal of the clitoris and/or the labia minora and/or majora.

Type 3 - Closing the vaginal opening leaving just a small hole for urine and menstrual blood to pass.

A type 2 can resemble a type 3 if the raw edges from type 2 are stitched, pressed or healed together naturally.

Type 4 - Any procedures that damage the external female genitalia.

All types of FGM are harmful and results in immediate and long term health risks.

The division between the different types is not always clear. There can easily be a mix of genital findings.

### Immediate health risks of FGM

Severe pain and shock, infection, excessive bleeding and death.

### Long term risks of FGM

Chronic vulvar pain and/or clitoral neuroma, causing pain when touching the area. Difficulty in passing urine and menstrual blood. Urine tract infections. Keloids and/or vulval cysts (epidermal inclusion cysts) causing itching and pain
and psychological consequences.

### Birth complication due to FGM

Prolonged 2nd stage of labour, perineal tears, newborn asphyxia, postpartum haemorrhage and instrumental delivery and a higher risk of caesarean section.
