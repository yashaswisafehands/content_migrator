This is a suggestion for malaria treatment

However, the patterns of resistance to antimalarial drugs vary across countries,
therefore a universal malaria treatment regime is not possible.

Follow your national guidelines.

# Indications and usage

Treatment of severe and cerebral malaria.

Used in treatment of uncomplicated Plasmodium falciparum malaria

# Dosage and administration

Loading dose: 20 mg salt/kg IV infusion or divided IM. Infusion rate should not be more than 5mg salt/kg per hour.

Maintenance: 10 mg salt/kg every 8 hours

Continue parenteral therapy for a minimum of 48 hours, then switch to oral quinine 10 mg/kg 8 hourly to complete a 7-day course (including parenteral days).

# Contraindications

None.

# Adverse reactions

Quinine is safe in pregnancy but often causes side effects like metallic taste, nausea, and tinnitus.

Hypoglycaemia is commonly seen, the blood sugar level should be measured frequently, and if low, 1 litre of 10% glucose should be given intravenously.

Uncommon adverse reactions are swallowing difficulties, vomiting, abdominal pain,
hepatitis, delirium, exaltation, and visual disturbances.

Rare adverse reactions are cardiac arrhythmia, or even heart arrest, respiratory depression, confusion, and renal toxicity.
