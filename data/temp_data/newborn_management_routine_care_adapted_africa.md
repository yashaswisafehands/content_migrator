# Chapter: APGAR Score

### APGAR Score

Give the newborn APGAR score 1 and 5 minutes after birth.

**APGAR 7-10:**Healthy

>

**APGAR 0-6:** Requires medical attention

[TableStart]
|                          | 0 points           | 1 point                       | 2 points      |
| ------------------------ | ------------------ | ----------------------------- | ------------- |
| Activity                 | Limp arms and legs | Arms and legs flexed          | Active        |
| Pulse                    | Absent             | Less than 100                 | Above 100     |
| Grimace                  | No response        | Grimace                       | Sneeze, cough |
| Appearance (skin colour) | Blue or pale       | Body pink, arms and legs blue | All pink      |
| Respiration              | Absent             | Slow, irregular               | Crying        |
| Total score              |                    |                               |               |
[TableEnd]

# Chapter: Initial Care

### Initial care for all newborns

All newborns need routine care after delivery.

Additional care is required for low birth weight.

Avoid separating mother from her newborn whenever possible and do not leave them unattented at any time.

- Assess the newborn immediately after delivery. Start a clock or note down the time.
- Upon delivery, dry and stimulate the newborn. If not crying well or has difficulty breathing proceed to neonatal resuscitation.
- Assess APGAR Score at 1 and 5 minutes.
- Keep warm in skin-to-skin contact with the mother to prevent hypothermia and promote breastfeeding.
- Monitor the newborn closely.  
- Check breathing and colour every 15 minutes.
- Check the cord for bleeding every 15 minutes, keep it clean and dry. If bleeding, retie it. 
- Give all newborns vitamin K 1 mg IM.
- Initiate breastfeeding as soon as possible within the first hour of birth and let the newborn feed on demand. 
- Examine the newborn, look for danger signs.
- Give vaccines according to national protocol. 
- Apply antibiotic eye ointment and chlorhexidine digluconate on the umbilical cord, if relevant in your local setting.
- Give vaccines according to national guidelines.
- Weigh the newborn, if less than 2.5 kg consider the newborn as having a low birth weight and at risk of complications such as low blood sugar, respiratory distress, and severe infections. Admit and monitor closely for danger signs, consider referral to a facility with advanced care. 
- See Low Birth Weight module for further details.
- If clinically stable, encourage Kangaroo Mother Care (KMC): continuous and prolonged skin-to-skin contact with the mother or another caregiver. Initiate at the hospital and continue at home.

At discharge, ask the mother to return with her newborn in case of any danger signs.
