# Chapter: Instructions to patients

## Place of stay

- Isolate in well-ventilated room with cross ventilation
- Do not share personal items with other people in the household
- Ensure cleaning of surfaces in the room that are touched often with 1% hypochlorite solutions.

## Respiratory and Hand Hygiene

- Use triple layer medical mask
- Discard mask after 8 hours of use or earlier if they become wet disinfecting it with 1% Sodium Hypochlorite
- Respiratory etiquettes always
- Frequent hand washing with soap and water for at least 40 seconds or clean with alcohol-based sanitizer
- Take rest and drink lot of fluids to maintain adequate hydration

# Instructions to healthcare providers/Care provider

- Wear a triple layer medical mask.
- N95 mask may be considered when in the same room.
- Front portion of the mask should not be touched during use
- Discard the mask after use and perform hand hygiene after disposal of the mask.
- Use disposable gloves while handling the patient
- Perform hand hygiene before and after removing gloves
- Food must be provided to the patient in his/her room
- Utensils and dishes used by the patient should be cleaned with soap/detergent and water wearing gloves
- The utensils and dishes may be re-used.

# Self-monitoring at home

## Monitoring chart

- Days of symptoms and time (every 4 hours)
- Temperature
- Heart rate (from pulse oximeter)
- SpO2 % (from pulse oximeter)
- Feeling: (better, same, worse)
- Breathing: (better, same, worse)

## Treatment for patients with mild /asymptomatic disease in home  isolation

- Warm water gargles or take steam inhalation twice a day
- If fever not controlled with a maximum dose of Tab. Paracetamol 650 mg four times a day, consider other drugs like NSAIDs (ex: Tab. Naproxen 250 mg twice a day)
- Prone positioning: Prone positioning feasible in pregnant as well as postpartum patients
- Including the recently delivered
- Padding and/or support devices (eg, pillows, padding from the operating room, etc) may need to be utilized
- Passive prone positioning:
- Patient is not intubated
- Positions herself in either the lateral decubitus (typically for ~2 hours in each position) or
- Fully prone position may aid in patient comfort
- Theoretically help avoid intubation.

## For how long should the mother/baby precautions be continued at home in mothers tested positive?

Asymptomatic mothers: Upto 10 days of testing positive on routine screening

**Symptomatic mothers:** When all the following are met

- At least 10 days have passed since symptoms first appeared (up to 20 days if they have more severe to critical illness or are severely immunocompromised)
- At least 3 days have passed since their last fever without the use of antipyretics
- Symptoms have improved

## Indications for admission to ICU

- Inability to maintain sPO2 ≥95% with supplemental oxygen/rapidly escalating supplemental oxygen need
- Hypotension (MAP<65mmHg) despite fluid resuscitation)
- Evidence of new end-organ dysfunction (eg, altered mental status, renal insufficiency, hepatic insufficiency, cardiac dysfunction, etc.).

## When should the COVID positive mother be discharged?

The decesion should be based on need for monitoring and hospital care, and availability of home isolation facilities.

- For asymptomatic patients: 24-48 hrs after a normal vaginal delivery, Day 3 or 4 of Caesarean section
