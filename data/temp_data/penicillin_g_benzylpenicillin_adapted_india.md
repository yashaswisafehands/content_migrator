# Chapter: Indications and Usage

Penicillin is used to treat a wide range of infections like pneumonia, pleural empyema, erysipelas, endocarditis, osteomyelitis, and arthritis caused by bacteria sensitive to penicillin like streptococcus pneumoniae, beta-haemolytic streptococcus, in some instances staphylococcus aureus (though most strains are resistant), clostridium species (except clostridium difficile), bacillus anthracis, corynebacterium diphtheria, actinomyces, certain strains of neisseria gonorrhoea, neisseria meningitides, treponema pallidum, pasteurella multocida, and borrelia burgdorferi.

# Dosage and administration

## Adults:

1–2 million international units (IU) every 6 hours IV or (less effective) IM or orally.
In severe infections, higher doses may be needed. The daily maximum dose is 20 million IU.

With severe infections, higher doses may be needed: the daily maximum dose is 12 g (20 million IU) of penicillin G.

**Intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis for GBS:** 

Penicillin G 5 million IU IV every 6 hours during labour.

If labour lasts longer than 18 hours:
 
Penicillin G 1 million IU orally every 8 hours until birth may be considered.

##

**Under 7 days:**

Penicillin G 50.000 IU/kg IV every 12 hours.

**Above 7 days:**

Penicillin G 50.000 IU/kg IV every 6 hours.

# Contraindications

Allergy to penicillin.

Severe allergy (type 1) to other B-lactam antibiotics (like cephalosporins).

# Adverse reactions

Common adverse reactions are a skin rash.

Uncommon adverse reactions are urticaria.

Rare adverse reactions are haemolytic anaemia and anaphylactic reactions.
