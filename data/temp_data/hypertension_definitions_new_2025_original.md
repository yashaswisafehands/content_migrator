# Chapter: Hypertension in pregnancy: Types and definitions

Hypertension is persistent high blood pressure at or above 140/90. In pregnancy, it can take different forms:

## 1. Chronic hypertension

- Before pregnancy or before 20 weeks gestation.
- May increase the risk of pre-eclampsia.

## 2. Gestational hypertension

- New-onset hypertension after 20 weeks gestation.
- Typically resolves after birth. 
- Can develop into pre-eclampsia

## 3. Pre-eclampsia

- New-onset hypertension after 20 weeks gestation WITH protein in the urine.

## 4. Severe pre-eclampsia

Blood pressure ≥ 160/110 mmHg OR any of these symptoms:

- Persistent severe headache (behind the eyes)
- Visual disturbances (flashing lights/blurry vision)
- Persistent upper abdominal pain (liver area)
- Respiratory distress (pulmonary oedema)
- Decreased urine output (<400 mL/day)
- Nausea and vomiting
- Hyperreflexia or clonus

## 5. Eclampsia

- Seizures during pregnancy related to high blood pressure.
- Most cases occur in women with pre-eclampsia.
- Can be life-threatening. 
- In the instance of seizures, consider the possibility of other causes (cerebral malaria, meningitis).
