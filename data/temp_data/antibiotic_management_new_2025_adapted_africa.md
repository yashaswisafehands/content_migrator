# Chapter: Antibiotic treatment for newborns

## Clinical signs of infection in newborns (danger signs):

- Not feeding well or not feeding at all. 
- No movement or only moves when stimulated. 
- High body temperature (≥ 38 °C) or low body temperature (< 35.5 °C). 
- Severe chest indrawing. 
- Convulsions. 
- Fast breathing (≥ 60 breaths per minute in newborns aged 0-6 days, and ≥ 50 breaths per minute in newborns above 7 days old). 
- Drowsiness or unconsciousness. 
- Grunting. 
- Central cyanosis. 
- Severe jaundice. 
- Severe abdominal distension.

> Immediate Action: 

Treat immediately with antibiotics with any clinical signs of infection or if clinical suspicion arises:

**First dose:**

- Ampicillin 50 mg/kg AND gentamicin 5 mg/kg IM/IV (Gentamicin 3 mg/kg IM/IV if birthweight < 2.5 kg)

**Futher treatment protocol:**

- **In 1st week of life:** Ampicilling 50 mg/kg IM/IV every 12 hours, AND Gentamicin 5 mg/kg IM/IV once daily. 
- **After 1st week of life:** Ampicillin 50 mg/kg IM/IV every 8 hours, AND Gentamicin 7.5 mg/kg IM/IV once daily. 
- Duration: 7-10 days

**Note:**If ampicillin IV is not available, consider:

- **Oral amoxicillin:** 50 mg/kg/dose every 12 hours, AND 
- **IM/IV gentamicin**: 5 mg/kg (1st week) or 7.5 mg/kg (after 1st week) once daily. 
- **Duration:**7 days (full course) **OR**2 days gentamicin + 7 days amoxicillin if 7-day gentamicin is not feasible. 
- Refer to higher-level hospital.

**If Staphylococcal infection is suspected**, treat with cloxallin IM/IV AND gentamicin IM/IV for at least 10 days.

If **meningitis is suspected**, treat with: ampicillin OR cefotaxime OR ceftriaxone IM/IV AND gentamicin for at least 3 weeks.

**If fast breathing is the ONLY sign of infection in newborns older than 7 days and there are no other clinical signs of sepsis, these newborns can be treated with:**

- Amoxicillin 50 mg/kg/dose orally every 12 hours for 7 days.

**Close Monitoring:** Follow-up on days 4 and 7 to monitor the clinical progress.
