# Chapter: BP 140-150/90/100

### Management of Gestational Hypertension

Send home

Schedule follow up appointments in 3-4 days and instruct in danger signs:

•	Severe headache, 
•	Blurring, 
•	Epigastric pain,
•	Breathing difficulty

Encourage her to rest at least 2 hours a day and 8 hours of sleep at night

Encourage her to take a normal diet

Do not restrict her intake of salt and fluids

Advise her to go for institutional delivery at 37 completed weeks.

# Chapter: BP 150-160/100/110

### Management of Pre-eclampsia without Severe Features

Hospitalize the woman, reassure, and advice rest

Start anti-hypertensive agent (as per prescription) if:

SBP ≥ 150 mmHg and or

DBP ≥ 100 mmHg

Monitor BP and urine output

Investigate – CBC with peripheral smear and platelet count, LFT, KFT and fundus exam

# Chapter: Severe Preeclampsia

### Management of Severe preeclampsia

Hospitalize the woman urgently and stabilize her

**To prevent eclampsia:**Give Inj. MgSo4

> REMEMBER

Start Anti-hypertensive drugs if BP ≥150/100 mmHg

Initiate therapy for acute hypertensive crisis if SBP ≥ 160 or DBP ≥ 110mmHg

Keep record of BP as Nifedipine may cause sudden hypotension

Continue monitor the BP:

1. Every 15 min for 2 hours after stabilisation
2. Every 30 min for 1 hour
3. Every hour if woman is in labour
4. Every 4 hours if woman is not in labour

If severe features persist: Plan referral for delivery within 24 hours.

###

At blood pressure between 150-160/100-110 start **oral antihypertensives** for example:

**Labetatol**starting dose: **200 mg orally every 6-12 hours**.

OR

**Methyldopa** starting dose: **250 mg orally every 6-8 hours**.

Persisting blood pressure above 159 systolic or above 109 diastolic must be treated with IV.

See drug list for more details.

Assess the blood pressure every 15 minutes and treat with IV according to the suitable **antihypertensives** in the drug list until the blood pressure is below 160/110.

See drug list for more details.
