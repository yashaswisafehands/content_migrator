# Chapter: Post-Procedure Care After Vacuum Extraction

**Immediately after the procedure:**

- Monitor the woman’s vital signs.
- Assess for abdominal pain.
- Observe vaginal bleeding; it should gradually decrease.
- Check for nausea or vomiting.

![Image](a8a61670075145ad98f3c05d038f0bba)

**Before discharge from the facility:**

- Ensure the woman feels well and her vital signs are stable.
- Offer contraceptive counselling and provide contraception if desired.
- Address other reproductive health needs such as anaemia, RTIs and HIV

Provide medications:

- Pain relief with analgesics or NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen)
- Antibiotics, if indicated 
- Anti-D injection (50 mcg) for Rh-negative women 
- Iron–folic acid (IFA) tablets for at least three months

Advise on self-care post-discharge:

- Resume normal diet the same day
- Avoid strenuous activity for 3 days 
- Avoid vaginal douching, preferably avoid intercourse for a week or until bleeding stops

Explain danger signs requiring urgent care:

- Excessive or prolonged bleeding
- Severe abdominal pain or persistent cramping 
- Vomiting or fever 
- Signs of physical decline: low blood pressure, dizziness, fainting, shortness of breath

# Chapter: Follow-up Care

Schedule a follow-up visit within 1–2 weeks after the procedure.

At the follow-up visit, assess:

![Image](70e9972920494a9688bab4073baa4480)

- General physical condition and vital signs
- Vaginal bleeding pattern
- Presence of fever, abdominal or pelvic pain, or cramps
- Resolution or persistence of pregnancy symptoms (e.g., nausea, breast tenderness) to rule out ongoing pregnancy

If contraception was not chosen earlier, revisit contraceptive options with the woman.
