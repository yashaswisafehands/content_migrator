# Chapter: Safe oxygen management

Recognizing Hypoxaemia: 
Hypoxaemia (low levels of oxygen in the blood) is a life-threatening and can occur due to birth asphyxia, meconium aspiration, respiratory distress syndrome, severe pneumonia or sepsis.

**Use pulse oximetry whenever possible.**

It is recommended for determining the presence of hypoxaemia and for guiding administration of oxygen therapy to neonates.

![Image](09464c55140f4fd5992ba140738a8c80)

## Target saturation when giving oxygen therapy:

- Maintain saturation in between 91-96%.

**Signs of hypoxaemia:**

- Central cyanosis 
- Nasal flaring,  
- Inability to drink/feed (due to respiratory distress)  
- Grunting
- Drowsy and/or lethargic
- Severe lower chest wall indrawing
- Respiratory rate >70/min
- Head nodding

## When to give oxygen:

- Oxygen saturation ≤ 90%

**Oxygen delivery methods:**

Preferred method:

- Nasal prongs for newborns
- Avoid face masks and head boxes.
- Note: Consider CPAP if available or refer the newborn to a facility with CPAP.

Standard oxygen flow rates: Neonates: 0.5-1 L/min (max 1 L/min without humidifcation. 
1-2	L/min for infants 
1-4 L/min for older children

## Monitoring:

- Use pulse oximetry, when available.
- Full-term newborn: normal SpO2 >88% in first hours, after that between 94-100%
- Preterm (<32 weeks): Maintain 88% and 95% (avoid > 95%, to prevent retinopathy). 
- Discontinue oxygen: When stable at   >90% for at least 15 min in room air.
- Discharge only when: Stable at >90% for at least 24 hours and all danger signs have resolved.
