# Chapter: Measure the symphysis-fundal height (SFH)

Make sure the woman's bladder is empty.

She should lay comfortably on her back.

Look for scars from previous caesarean sections or other operations.

![Image](cb8097b659a54ea8ab776696c351e257)

- Measure the symphysis-fundal height (SFH) (from 24 weeks).
- Gently palpate the woman's abdomen with one hand to determine the height of the fundus of the uterus.
- Hold one end of the tape measure at the top of the symphysis pubis and the top of the fundus (in centimetres). 
- For foetuses growing normally, the SFH should correspond to the number of weeks of gestation, with and allowance of a 2-cm difference either way.

![Image](5f6369b850794c418bc8b2b4e98fbea6)

# Chapter: Palpation of the foetus

There are four steps for palpating the foetus. These should be performed systematically.

## 1. The lie:

This may be longitudinal, transverse or oblique. Place both hands on the woman's abdomen and glide them across the uterus to determine the foetal lie.

![Image](280943dc596f4413af802268274fbe73)

## 2. Which part is present at the uterine fundus?

Use your fingers on both hands to palpate what part of the foetus is present at the fundus (breech or head). The head is hard and round, and moves and ballots easily. The breech is softer, triangular and continuous with the body.

![Image](1c4b77ffdeb347c7bd159acba89f76d6)

## 3. The position of the back:

Place your hands flat on either side of her abdomen. With one hand, gently palpate the uterus with small pushes, while the other hand provides counterpressure. Use this technique to locate the foetal back and work out which way the foetus facing.

![Image](a8e20537abee4b1d8ffe271c64d0a1ba)

## 4. The presenting part:

Gently use both hands flat either side of the lower abdomen, just above the symphysis pubis, to feel the presenting part. Assess wheater the presenting part is fixed in the pelivs or is loose.

![Image](7a688c102ac144f08f75d0d43baa9dcb)

As you are palpating assess the size of the foetus itself. Does the foetus have the size you would expect for the size and the gestation?

On assessment, if there are any abnormal findings and you suspect an increased risks due to, for example, multiple pregnancy or intra-uterine growth restriction, an ultrasound examination of the foetus is recommened where possible.

Ask the woman if her baby's movements are following their usual pattern.

# Chapter: Contractions

Note the frequency, duration and intensity of contractions, and how the woman is coping with them.

![Image](c6c8834ab1564fe788c552c2a5ce003d)

1. Place a hand on her abdomen to feel the contractions. Notice when they begin and end. 
2. Note down their length, frequency and intensity over 10 minutes.

In the first stage of the active phase of labour, there should be regular, painful contractions every 3-5 minutes that last 45-60 seconds. This means 2-3 contractions in 10 minutes.

As labour progresses, contractions will intensify, lasting about 60-90 seconds with a 30 second-2 minute rest in between. The woman should not have more than 5 contractions per 10 minutes.
