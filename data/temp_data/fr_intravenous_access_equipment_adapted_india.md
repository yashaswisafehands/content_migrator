# Chapter: Usage

Intravenous access is used to administer fluids and drugs.

**Equipment & other requirements:**
•	Antiseptic solution to clean the surrounding skin.
•	Syringes (1 to 2ml) 
•	Intravenous cannulas
•	Venflons (24 & 26 gauge)
•	A stasis hose/band
•	Tapes

# Chapter: Practical information

## Intravenous Access - Practical information

The grey (gauge 16) and green (gauge 18) cannulas have a large diameter. They help to administer fluids into the veins quickly.

## Newborns:

When inserting an intravenous cannula in the neonate, locate a suitable vein to insert a 21 or 23 gauge cannula or butterfly needle.

![Image](daae3c74d19f40789d66cde60853b0ab)

## Peripheral vein:

- Select the vein (dorsum of hand/foot).
- Wash hands, dry and wear gloves.
- Prepare skin by cleaning with spirit, povidone iodine and spirit, let dry between applications.
- An assistant should hold the limb proximally to make the vein prominent.
- Pierce skin distal to the intended site of puncture.
- Insert needle into the vein (feeling of give way).
- Ensure free flow, thread the needle further up into the vein.
- Secure the intracath by adhesive tape.
- Secure splint if needed (generally not needed).
- Flush the cannula with 0.5ml normal saline.
- Inject fluid/medications.
- Check distal limb for adequacy of circulation.

![Image](e7f3d120c4a84f46b5588c6c63c5873b)

##

These are often used in children under 2 years of age.

- Find a suitable scalp vein (usually in the midline of the forehead, the temporal area or above or behind the ear).
- Shave the area if necessary and clean the skin with an antiseptic solution. An assistant should use a finger to occlude the vein proximal to the insertion site. Fill a syringe and leave the end of the tubing open. Insert the butterfly needle as described above. 
- Care should be taken not to cannulate an artery, which is recognized by palpation. If there is a pulsatile spurting of blood, withdraw the needle and apply pressure until the bleeding stops; then look for the vein.
