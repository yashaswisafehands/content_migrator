# Chapter: Manual Removal of Placenta

### Emergency referral for manual removal of placenta

> EMERGENCY!

Do not transfer if emergency is not controlled!

Never transfer a woman with uncontrolled bleeding, retained placenta or shock.

Following emergency management, when the woman is in a stable condition, discuss the decision to transfer the woman and her companions.

Quickly organize transport and possible financial aid.

Inform the referral centre.

Accompany the woman and her newborn.

It is crucial that the woman is accompanied by a health worker who can manage an emergency situation if it occurs during transportation.

Consider bringing a relative who can donate blood.

### Bring

Referral note.

## Essential emergency drugs:

- **Oxytocin** and **ergometrine**(alternatively **misoprostol**) for treatment of post-partum haemorrhage.
- **Hydralazine** for IV treatment of high blood pressure
- **Magnesium sulphate** for eclampsia
- **Calcium gluconate** for intoxication with **magnesium sulphate**
- **Ampicillin**,**gentamicin**and  **metronidazole**for treatment and prevention of infections
- **Diazepam** for pain relief
- **Phenobarbital** for convulsions in the newborn.
- **Adrenaline** for cardiac arrest in the newborn.

## Supplies:

- Towels
- IV-equipment + IV fluids
- Blood pressure monitor
- Adult and newborn ambu bag with appropriate mask sizes
- Suction bulb
- Syringes of different sizes
- Examination and sterile gloves

### During Transport

Observe vital signs and level of consciousness.

In case of excessive vaginal bleeding apply bimanual compression of the uterus.

If the bleeding does not stop apply aortic compression (can be maintained up to 6 hours).

If the woman’s condition allows:

Document administration and times of any IV fluids and medications, as well as the woman's condition.
