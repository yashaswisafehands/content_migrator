# Chapter: Antibiotic prophylaxis

Antibiotics should be administered only when there is a clear medical indication.

In most cases, antibiotic prophylaxis is not necessary in normal labour and birth.

Antibiotic administration is recommended for women with a:

- Temperature above 38°C before or during labour  
- History of foul-smelling discharge  
- Third- or fourth-degree perineal tear  
- Elective or emergency caesarean section  
- Manual removal of the placenta  
- Assisted vaginal birth (either by vacuum extractor or forceps) 
- Confirmed vaginal colonization with group B Streptococcus in labour  
- And preterm prelabour rupture of membranes (PPROM)
- Prolonged labour lasting more than 24 hours
- Obstructed labour

No antibiotic prophylaxis is required in the case of prelabour rupture of membranes (PROM) at or near term, except when there is more than 18 hours between rupture of membranes and delivery. This is called “prolonged rupture of membranes”.

For information on dosage and administration of drugs, see the specified drug list.
