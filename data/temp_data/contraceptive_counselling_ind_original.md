# Chapter: Types of Counselling

Contraceptive counselling is a client-centred process that helps individuals and couples make informed choices about family planning.

## The contraceptive counselling, using the GATHER approach is carried out in three stages:

**Initial / General Counselling –**is done before choosing a contraceptive method to understand the client’s reproductive needs, share various contraceptive method options, and support informed decision-making.

**Method-Specific Counselling –** is provided before or immediately after adapting contraceptive method to explain correct use, effectiveness, advantages, limitations, side effects and their management, protection against RTIs/STIs.

**Follow-Up Counselling –** is conducted during return visits to review the client’s experience, encourage continued use, and address any concerns or difficulties of client if experienced.

# Chapter: GATHER Approach

### G - Greet

![Image](466fafeb9b76404194599d9efebb449a)

- Welcome the client respectfully, introduce self, offer a seat, and ensure she feels comfortable.
- Provide privacy, confirm basic details, explain confidentiality, and ask the purpose of her visit.
- Use simple language, ask open-ended questions, and involve family members with her consent.

### A – Ask

- Explore client’s knowledge about return of fertility and benefits of spacing pregnancies, her needs, reproductive goals, past experiences with contraception, health status, and concerns.
- Rules out pregnancy by asking relevant questions to be reasonably sure that the woman is not pregnant (for PPFP).
- Display the counselling kit with contraceptives and flip book and ask if client is interested in using any particular method

### T – Tell

- Provide brief, relevant, accurate information on suitable contraceptive methods—usage, effectiveness, common side effects, STI/HIV protection.
- Use counselling aids like flipbooks, brochures, for better understanding. 
- Address any myths or misconceptions.

### H – Help

- Assists the client in choosing a method that best suits their needs or gives her additional information that she needs to make a decision.
- Support her choice and explain the next steps for receiving the selected method.

### E – Explain

- Assess if the chosen method is medically safe for her (use MEC wheel) anddemonstrate correct use of the chosen method.
- Explain about the chosen method: type, use, effectiveness, return of fertility, impact on breastfeeding, advantages limitations, common side effects.
- Provide the chosen method if available or if not inform the client when, where it will be available or refer her to the nearest facility.
- Explain the warning signs, what to do if problems occur, and when to return.

### R – Return

- If undecided, encourage discussion with family and plan a follow-up visit.
- Schedule the next visit and advise her to return anytime if needed or for more supplies.
- Document all relevant details in the woman's chart.

# Chapter: Key Information For Women

![Image](dea05adca01445cbac0eac0e0ee8e439)

Key information for women choosing a contraceptive method should include:

- Type of method – e.g. is it temporary or permanent? Hormonal or non-hormonal?
- Effectiveness – e.g. what are the chances of pregnancy with correct use? 
- Side-effects, health benefits, health risks + complications.
- How to use or what to expect from a procedure – e.g. what practical information does the woman need to know?
- When to return i.e. for follow-up, resupply or if they have questions or concerns.
- How to prevent HIV and other STIs through dual protection.
