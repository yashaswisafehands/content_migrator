# Chapter: Subdermal Contraceptive Implant

Single rod subdermal implant is a progestogen-only contraceptive device, which comes in a sterile, preloaded disposable applicator with needle for single use.

It is inserted subdermally on inner side of the non-dominant upper arm.

The slow release of progesterone hormone in the woman’s body provides contraceptive action, with effectiveness upto 3 years.

It works by preventing ovulation and thickening the cervical mucus, blocking sperm from reaching an egg.

They provide very effective (99.9%) contraception for 3 years, depending on the type of implant. For every 100 women using implants, there will only be less than 1 that gets pregnant in one year (1 per 1,000 women).

Protection against sexually transmitted infections (STIs):**None**

Return of fertility after removal: **No delay**

![Image](b83a94ac712d45b9b9d28d8107141b4a)

## Insertion

An implant is ideally inserted in the first seven days of the menstrual cycle but can be inserted any time if you are reasonably certain that the woman is not pregnant. It can be inserted immediately after delivery irrespective of breastfeeding status.

A trained provider performs a minor surgical procedure to place the rod under the skin on the inner side of a woman’s arm.

**The applicator consists of:**

- **Transparent protective cap or needle shield:** covers the needle and is removed prior to insertion.
- **Textured area:** This is rough dotted area on applicator for facilitating proper grip and  hold at this point while inserting the Implant.
- **Slider:** It lies on top of the applicator. Once Implant is inserted, it is unlocked by pushing it slightly down and then pushed back to its full length. 
- **Bi-bevelled needle:** The needle has two bevels at the tip - one to insert at a 30° angle and the other one to lift the skin after insertion.

![Image](8acb16704e23479e96025b5a49ff01e3)

## Removal

A trained provider performs a minor surgical procedure to remove the implant(s).

![Image](b745f62ef558470bafe21a40dd8bed6b)

## Women with HIV

Women who are living with HIV or are on antiretroviral therapy (ART) can safely use implants. However, medications for HIV such as efavirenz may reduce the effectiveness of implant. Therefore, women with the implant taking ART should also use condoms for dual protection.

## Some women like implants as they

- Do not require the user to do anything once inserted
- Protect against pregnancy effectively
- Are long-lasting and reversible
- Do not interfere with sex

## Why some women may not like implants

- Side effects
- They require small procedures for insertion and removal
- No STI protection

## Side effects (from common to least common)

Changes in bleeding patterns, headaches, abdominal pain, acne, weight changes, breast tenderness, dizziness, mood changes and nausea.

Some may experience ovarian cysts (usually resolve without treatment)

## Known health benefits

- Helps to protect against risks of pregnancy, including ectopic pregnancy, and symptomatic pelvic inflammatory disease.
- May protect against iron-deficiency anaemia
- Reduces ectopic pregnancy risk

## Known health risks

None

## Complications

- Infection and abscess formation at insertion site (uncommon)
- Difficult removal (common)
- Expulsion of implant (rare)
- Heart disease, Deep vein thrombosis and stroke (rare)

# Chapter: Intra Uterine Contraceptive Device (IUCD)

The copper-bearing intrauterine contraceptive device (IUCD) is a small, flexible plastic device with copper coils and barium sulfate. 

Copper ions decrease sperm motility by altering the uterine and tubal fluid environment, thus preventing sperm from reaching the fallopian tubes and fertilizing the egg, and also prevents implantation.

There are two types of IUCD:

1. **IUCD 380 A**- Effective for 10 years 
2. **IUCD 375**- Effective for 5 years

## Effectiveness

For every 100 women that use an IUCD, less than 1 will become pregnant within the first year (6 to 8 pregnancies out of 1,000 women).

All types of IUCDs have two strings, or threads, tied to them. The strings hang through the cervix into the vagina.

Protection against sexually transmitted infections (STIs): **None**

Return of fertility after removal: **No delay**

![Image](29eea4e669ed41ec908db2b1a6091ce7)

## Insertion

A specifically trained health care provider inserts it into a woman’s uterus through her cervix.

Teach the woman how to identify symptoms of pelvic infection, string problems, and that delayed menstrual periods suggest possible pregnancy.

## Follow-up

A follow-up appointment is recommended after her first menstrual bleeding or 6 weeks after insertion, whichever is earlier.

## Some women like IUCDs as they:

- Prevent pregnancy effectively and reversible
- Are long-lasting 
- Are private (usually no one else can tell a woman is using contraception, sometimes a partner may feel the strings during sex)
- One-time cost-effective procedure 
- Safe for use in breastfeeding women

## Why some women may not like IUCDs:

- Side effects
- No STI protection
- Insertion and removal can be uncomfortable or painful

## Side effects

Some users reported changes in bleeding patterns, especially within the first few months (typically more irregular, longer and heavier bleeding, with more painful cramps), discomfort and backache

## Known health benefits

- Helps prevent pregnancy
- May help protect against endometrial and cervical cancers
- Reduces the risk of ectopic pregnancy

## Known health risks

- Heavier bleeding may contribute to anaemia if a woman already has low haemoglobin.
- Risk of pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) if woman has chlamydia or gonorrhoea at the time of IUD insertion (rare)

## Complications

- Puncturing (perforation) of the wall of the uterus by the IUCD or an instrument used for insertion, which usually heals without treatment. (rare)
- Miscarriage, preterm birth, or infection in the rare case that the woman becomes pregnant with the IUCD in place. (rare)
