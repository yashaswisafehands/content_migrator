# Chapter: Measure the symphysis-fundus (SF) height

**Before performing abdominal examinations:**

Explain procedure and gain verbal consent 

Ensure privacy.

Ask the woman to empty the bladder 

Assist the woman to lie down comfortably in a supine position with legs partially flexed 

Stand on the right side of woman while doing examinations 

Wash hands and warm them 

Centralize the uterus and look for scars on the abdomen.

Ensure uterus is not contracted.

**Measure the symphysis-fundus (SF) height**

Assess foetal growth after 24 weeks.

Make sure the legs are straight.

Look for scars (from previous cesarean sections or other operations).

**To mark the fundus;**

![Image](378703852c5c4741bee3d7210c5bf307)

- Divide the abdomen into parts by imaginary lines.
- Keep the horizontal line passing through the umbilicus as the centre and divide the lower and upper abdomen into three parts, with two imaginary equidistant lines.
- Use the ulnar border of your left hand.
- Palpate from the xiphisternum downward until you feel first resistance.
- Measure the distance in centimeter between the upper boarder of the symphysis pubis and the top of the fundus using a measuring tape.
- The position of fundus at the level of umbilicus corresponds to 24 weeks of gestation.
- The gestational age (in weeks) corresponds to the fundal height (in cm) after 24 weeks of gestation With a deviation of+/- 1 to 2cm.
- If the fundal height is  ≥ 3 cm than the pregnancy week, it might be multiple pregnancy, large baby or polyhydramnios.
- If fundal height is ≤ 3 cm compared to the pregnancy week, it might be growth restriction, transverse lie, oligohydramnios.

# Chapter: Palpation of the fetus

There are four grips for palpating the foetus. These should be performed systematically.

## The lie of the foetus:

This may be longitudinal, transverse or oblique. Place both hands on the abdomen and let them glide along the uterus to determine the lie of the foetus.

![Image](cf9a92e19da74a8f81ea9ce470210252)

## 1. Fundal grip : Which part is present at the top of the uterus?

Palpate the fundus with the fingers of both hands in order to discover which part of the foetus is present (breech or head). The head feels hard and round and is easily movable and ballotable. The breech feels soft, triangular and continuous with the body.

![Image](cdbcf251444d44e78f5ac570b15e2a46)

## 2. Lateral grip: The position of the foetal limbs and back

Place one hand on each side of the abdomen. With one hand you give small pushes against the uterus while the other hand provides counterpressure. You will now be able to feel where foetal limbs and back is.

![Image](adee094122274177abd96364334a67ae)

## 3. Superficial pelvic grip: The presenting part of the foetus

Place the widely spread right hand over the symphysis pubis with the ulnar border touching it, apply gentle pressure to feel foetal parts—head feels hard and globular, breech feels soft and irregular; if the head doesn’t move side to side, it is engaged.

![Image](ac76d5e904ad4b7d84b5b0e242f1aeca)

**4. Deep pelvic grip: To confirm engagement of the presenting part**

Place palms on the uterine sides with fingers pointing downward to assess for multiple foetuses and identify the presenting part—head feels firm, round, and ballotable; diverging fingers indicate engagement, converging indicates no engagement.

As you are palpating the foetus, remember to assess the size of the foetus itself. Does the foetus have the size you would expect for the size of the uterus and the duration of the pregnancy?

If any of your findings are abnormal and you suspect things like a multiple pregnancy or intra-uterine growth restriction due to a small SF measurement, the woman should have an ultrasound examination if possible.

It is also important to ask the woman if she is feeling foetal movements as usual.

# Chapter: Contractions

You should note down frequency, duration and intensity of the contractions as well as how the woman is coping.

![Image](a959582b83ab4659b18b5a5bed735389)

1. Place a hand on the abdomen to feel when the contractions start and end. Take the time at the beginning and end of the contractions. 
2. Note down the length, frequency and intensity of the contractions that occur in a period of 10 minutes.

In the first stage of the active phase there will be regular painful contractions lasting about 45-60 seconds each with 3-5 minutes in between. This means 2-3 contractions in 10 minutes.

As labour progresses contractions will intensify and last about 60-90 seconds each with a 30 second- 2-minute rest in between.
