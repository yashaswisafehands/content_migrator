# Chapter: Prevention and early intervention

> Preventive measures are essential to reduce risks for mothers and newborns. These guidelines cover respiratory distress, severe infections, and low blood sugar to ensure better outcomes.

## Respiratory distress

With premature labour between 24 and 34 weeks' gestational or estimated birth weight below 2 kg, give the mother 

**Betamethasone**12 mg IM twice, 12 hours apart,

OR 

**Dexamethasone**6 mg four times, 12 hours apart.

## Severe infection

Consider severe infection if:

- Membranes rupture before 37 weeks' gestation or more than 18 hours before birth.
- The mother has a fever higher than 38˚C before or during labour.
- Amniotic fluid is purulent or foul smelling.
- The mother screens positive for group B streptococcus:

**Give the mother:** 
Ampicillin 2 g IV every 6 hours. 

AND

**Give the newborn:** 
Ampicillin 50 mg/kg IV/IM twice daily and gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV/IM once daily for at least 2 days.

Note: If the mother has HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis or tuberculosis, follow national guidelines for treatment of the newborn.

## Low blood sugar

If a newborn is asphyxiated or shows any danger signs:

1. Keep the newborn warm in skin-to-skin contact with the mother to prevent hypothermia and promote breastfeeding. 
2. Initiate breastfeeding as soon as possible within the first hour of birth and let the newborn feed on demand.
3. If unable to suck give expressed milk 10 ml/kg by cup or nasogastric tube. 
4. If feeding is not possible or the newborn vomits, give **glucose 10%**according to the Action Card “Feeding and fluid management”.
