# Chapter: Usage

### Nasogastric tube - Usage

A nasogastric (or NG) tube enables access to the stomach and its contents.

NG tubes can be used for enteral feeding of infants.

They can also be used for gastric drainage, gastric decompression, gastric aspiration for taking a sample of gastric contents, and to provide a passage into the gastrointestinal (or GI) tract.

All necessary equipment should be available, prepared and assembled prior to inserting a nasogastric tube.

Basic equipment includes:

- Personal protective equipment
- NG tube
- Catheter tip irrigation 60 ml syringe
- Water-soluble lubricant, (preferably **2% Xylocaine jelly**. Otherwise, use water) 
- Adhesive tape
- Stethoscope
- pH indicator strips

# Chapter: Practical information

### Nasogastric tube - Practical information

### Insertion of a nasogastric tube:

- Hold the tip of the tube against the child’s nose and measure the distance from the nose to the ear lobe, then to the xiphisternum (epigastrium). Mark the tube at this point.

- Hold the infant firmly. Lubricate the tip of the catheter, and insert it directly into one nostril, advancing it slowly. It should easily pass down into the stomach without resistance. When the measured distance is reached, tape the tube in place to the infant's cheek.

- Aspirate a small amount of stomach contents with a syringe to confirm that the tube is in place and if possible, check with pH to ensure it is acidic (blue litmus paper will turn pink). If no fluid is obtained, inject a small amount of air through the NG tube while auscultating the upper abdomen to listen for a whooshing sound.

- If you are uncertain about the NG tubes placement, withdraw it and start again.

- Once the tube is in place, fix a 20 ml syringe to the end of the tube (without its plunger), and pour breatsmilk, artificial formula, or fluid into the syring, allowing it to flow by gravity.

- If oxygen therapy by nasopharyngeal catheter is required at the same time, pass both tubes down the same nostril and keep the other nostril patent by wiping away crusts and secretions, or pass the feeding tube through the mouth.
