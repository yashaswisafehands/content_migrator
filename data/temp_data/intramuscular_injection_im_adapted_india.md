# Chapter: Intramuscular injection (IM)

## Usage

Intramuscular injections are used to administer drugs deep into the muscle. 

This allows the medication to be absorbed into the bloodstream quickly.

Equipment for intramuscular injections should be located in the delivery room.

It should be available at all health facilities for adults as well as newborns.

**Equipment includes:**

- IM medication ampoule/vial
- Sterile Syringe and needle (single use)
- Large-bore needle for withdrawing medication
- Cotton wool balls/ alcohol swabs

# Chapter: Intramuscular injections

## Practical information

**Adults:**
The needle gauge recommended for intramuscular injection is 22–25-gauge needle (25-38mm).

Intramuscular injections should be administered at a 90-degree angle to the skin.

Intramuscular injections are often given in the following areas:
1.	Deltoid muscle
2.	Vastus lateralis muscle of the thigh
3.	Ventrogluteal muscle of the hip
4.	Dosrogluteal muscles of the buttocks

**Procedure:**

1. Explain to the client about procedure.
2. Wash your hands.
3. If surrounding skin is soiled, clean with soap and water or alcohol swab.
4. Check the correct drug, dose, and client.
5. Draw the medication up into the syringe using a large bore needle. 
6. Change to the 22–25-gauge needle, ensuring the correct length for an adult patient (25-38mm).
7. Remove air bubbles if any.
8. Provide pressure on skin around injection site with thumb and index finger. 
9. Inject the needle in located site with a quick thrust at 90 degree angle and withdraw the plunger before pushing the medicine to confirm the right site and to ensure the medicine is not injected directly into a blood vessel.
10. Push the plunger slowly to inject the medication for even distribution and to minimize discomfort.
11. Remove the needle and apply pressure to the injection site with cotton wool ball.
12. Dispose the needle and syringe in an appropriate bin as per the biomedical waste Management protocols.
13. Observe the injection site for bleeding, pain or signs of infection.
14. Record the date, dose, route and time of administration of medicine in client case sheets.

**Newborns:**
The needle gauge recommended for intramuscular injection for newborns is 22-25-gauge needle (16-25 mm).

Intramuscular injections should be administered at a 90-degree angle to the skin into the vastus lateralis muscle in the anterolateral thigh.

For K1-vitamin and vaccines, a 1 mL or 0.5 mL syringe is used to administer the medication.

**Procedure:**

1. Inform the parents about the procedure.
2. Wash your hands.
3. If surrounding skin is soiled, clean with soap and water or aseptic swab.
4. Check the correct drug, dose, and newborn.
5. Draw the medication up into the syringe using a large bore needle. 
6. Change to the 22-25-gauge needle, ensuring the correct length for a newborn patient (16-25mm).
7. Remove air bubbles if any. 
8. Secure the thigh gently. 
9. Inject the needle in located site with a quick thrust and withdraw the plunger before pushing the medicine to confirm the right site and to ensure the medicine is not injected directly into a blood vessel.
10. Slowly inject the medication for even distribution and to minimize discomfort. 
11. Remove the needle.
12. If blood is seen, apply light pressure with cotton wool ball.
13. Dispose the needle and syringe safely as according to health facility requirements.
14. Record the date, dose, route and time of administration of medicine in client case sheets/ MCP card.

If administering more than one IM injection, give one injection into each thigh.
