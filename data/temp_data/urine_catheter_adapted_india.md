# Chapter: Usage

### Urinary Catheterization - usage

Catheterize only when necessary. 

Emptying the bladder with a urinary catheter is indicated:

1. If a woman in labour fails to urinate spontaneously and the progress of labour is dissatisfactory, **OR**
2. Prior to applying vacuum extraction **OR**
3. In cases of postpartum haemorrhage, **AND**
4. In any woman in a critical state (sepsis, severe preeclampsia, eclampsia, bleeding, shock) must have an indwelling catheter until the condition improves and the hourly urine output should be at least 30 ml.

# Chapter: Indwelling Urinary Catheter

![Image](235f0d0bf87142b28931313c56dd0d19)

## Practical information

Indwelling Urinary Catheter is a catheter that is inserted into the bladder via the urethra and left in the bladder to drain urine.

All women in a critical condition (sepsis, severe pre-eclampsia, eclampsia, bleeding, shock) must have an indwelling urinary catheter to monitor urinary output. The hourly urine output should be at least 30 ml.

If less, the underlying cause should be treated and fluid replacement considered, although not more than 75 ml per hour (oral and IV) should be given for women with preeclampsia as the risk of fluid overload and pulmonary oedema increases.

## Procedure

1. Explain procedure and assemble all equipments.
2. Maintain privacy and ensure that she should be lying on her back with knees flexed and thighs relaxed.
3. If available, cover the woman in a sterile drape.
4. Perform hand  hygiene prior to the procedure and put sterile gloves.
5. Separate the labia with one hand and expose the urethral opening.
6. The area around the urethra should be cleaned twice with gauze swabs and antiseptic solution.
7. Lubricate the catheter. 
8. Insert the catheter into the urethral opening. 
9. Inflate the balloon slowly by using sterile water to reach the volume recommended on the catheter.
10. **ALWAYS** ensure urine is expelling before inflating the balloon.
11. Pull back the catheter slightly until resistance is felt and attach it to the drainage system with the urine bag.
12. Fix the catheter at upper anterior thigh to prevent expulsion.
13. Remove gloves, perform hand hygiene and record the date and time of procedure in case sheet and maintain input and out chart.

## Removal

**Procedure**

1. Explain the procedure to the woman.
2. Ensure privacy and have the woman lying on her back or sitting on a toilet. 
3. Perform hand hygiene and put on gloves. 
4. Deflate the balloon completely. 
5. Gently pull back to remove the catheter. 
6. Remove gloves and perform hand hygiene. 
7. Observe urine output after removal of the catheter.  
8. The woman should urinate spontaneously 4-6 hours after the removal.

# Chapter: Intermittent Urinary Catheter

## Practical Information

An intermittent urinary catheter is inserted at regular intervals or when urination is required but the woman is not able to empty her bladder herself.

## Procedure

1. Explain procedure and assemble all equipments. 
2. Maintain privacy and ensure that she should be lying on her back with knees flexed and thighs relaxed.
3. If available, cover the woman in sterile drape.
4. Perform hand hygiene prior to the procedure and put sterile gloves.
5. Separate the labia with one hand and expose urethral opening.
6. The area around the urethra should be cleaned twice with gauze swabs and antiseptic solution.
7. Lubricate catheter. 
8. Insert catheter into the urethral opening. 
9. Empty the woman’s bladder and remove the catheter. 
10. Remove gloves and perform hand hygiene. 
11. Note down the urine volume.
